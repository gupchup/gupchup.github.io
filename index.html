Projects

Rider homepage personalization

Uber · 2022 – 2025

The Uber Rides homepage was a static, one-size-fits-all surface whose contents were effectively hard-coded. This work replaced it with a personalized, ranked experience. The decisive architectural move was decoupling layout generation from rendering: once the server decides what to show and in what order, content can be onboarded and ranked independently of the client release cycle. Onboarding new content fell from roughly three months to about a week, the number of themed carousels the system could rank and serve grew several-fold, and the ranking path extended beyond the rides homepage to scheduled rides and Uber Eats products.

In production the serving path sustains over 5,000 queries per second at sub-50 millisecond latency and better than 99.99% reliability. Measured through controlled A/B experiments, the ranking service drove nine-figure incremental revenue.

  • Ranking model. A production deep-learning ranker combining real-time engagement signals with explicit exploration. Discovery versus repeat access is the central modeling problem on a homepage — a purely greedy ranker collapses onto what a rider already does and quietly stops surfacing anything new.
  • Multi-task learning. One architecture predicting both clicks and conversions, so competing business objectives are traded off inside the model rather than across separately tuned ones.
  • Cold start. LLM-generated embedding features to rank content with little or no engagement history — the case where collaborative signals are absent by construction.
  • Serving efficiency. Inference runtime cut 50% through reduced numerical precision and a caching layer, which kept the latency budget intact as the model grew.

ML speech enhancement and meeting quality

Microsoft Teams · 2019 – 2022

Machine learning applied to the audio path and the meeting experience in Teams, spanning the training-data pipeline, the models, and the compliance boundary they had to operate inside. Deep noise suppression and echo cancellation models need training and test audio that reflects real calls; obtaining it without violating the compliance boundary is the constraint the data work is built around.

  • AURA — privacy-preserving augmentation to improve test set diversity in speech enhancement. A semi-supervised pipeline builds training and test sets from real call audio while remaining inside the compliance boundary; training set diversity rose 50% and out-of-distribution coverage 84%. ICASSP 2023 · arXiv · code
  • Speech interruption analysis — a transformer-based classifier that categorizes interruptions to drive automatic hand-raise and hand-lower, improving meeting inclusiveness. 100 hours of speech collected and labeled by crowdsourcing, with explicit data-quality metrics. ACM Multimedia 2022
  • From cloud to client — taking the interruption model from a cloud service to on-device deployment, where the latency and footprint budgets are far tighter. ICASSP 2023 · arXiv

Replacing software constants with learned models

Microsoft Teams & Skype · 2015 – 2022

Real-time communication stacks are full of hand-tuned operational constants — buffer sizes, thresholds, timeouts — chosen once and rarely revisited. This line of work replaced those constants with context-aware models learned online against live traffic, applied across more than ten scenarios spanning audio, video, transport, and payments. Models became the in-code default on the strength of A/B-validated Tier-1 metric wins.

  • Resonance — replacing software constants with context-aware models in real-time communication. NeurIPS ML for Systems 2020
  • Machine-learned audio jitter buffer — bandit methods tune the jitter buffer, which trades latency against concealment of late packets. Cut the poor-call rate by two percentage points absolute and became the default in the Skype and Teams clients; ranked in the top 1% of impactful A/B tests run in Microsoft's real-time communication organization.
  • Audio-quality classification — a classifier trained on network traces from real calls, with the architecture chosen against an explicit accuracy-versus-latency tradeoff for real-time serving.

Experimentation and metrics for real-time media

Microsoft · 2014 – 2019

Tier-1 metric definitions and A/B testing practice for the audio organization, taking it from roughly three experiments per quarter to a hundred within three years. Running experiments at that rate on a real-time media stack surfaced measurement problems worth publishing on in their own right — telemetry from a call is lossy and the loss is not random, which breaks the assumptions most experimentation platforms rest on.

  • Diagnosing sample ratio mismatch in online controlled experiments — when the traffic split itself is wrong, every downstream result is suspect. KDD 2019
  • Lumos — a library for diagnosing metric regressions in web-scale applications, accounting for population differences and invariant features. KDD 2020 · arXiv · code
  • Trustworthy experimentation under telemetry loss — how to keep experiment conclusions valid when the telemetry itself goes missing non-uniformly. CIKM 2018
  • Quality-of-experience surveys — analysis of problem tokens to rank factors impacting quality in VoIP applications, and on the design of problem-token questions.

Capacity forecasting during the Covid-19 surge

Microsoft · 2020

Teams usage grew by an order of magnitude in the first months of the pandemic, and cloud capacity has procurement lead times measured in weeks. A company-wide forecasting effort estimated the backend service capacity and bandwidth required to absorb the surge; the model underpinned securing a tenfold increase in service capacity ahead of the demand rather than behind it.

Environmental monitoring sensor networks

Johns Hopkins University · Ph.D. research

Sensor networks deployed in the field for months at a time are bound by power, and their data is only as good as the timestamps attached to it — clocks drift, nodes reboot, and the record has to be reconstructed after the fact. Doctoral work on data management for these deployments used machine learning to cut network power consumption by half while holding data accuracy above 99% at deployment scale.

  • Sundial — using sunlight to reconstruct global timestamps, recovering time from the light record when the clock is unreliable. arXiv
  • Phoenix — an epidemic approach to time reconstruction. arXiv
  • Tempo — an energy-harvesting mote resilient to power outages.
  • K2 — a system for campaign deployments of wireless sensor networks.
  • Wireless sensor networks for soil moisture monitoring SENSORNETS 2021 · arXiv
